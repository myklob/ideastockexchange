A need written at a different rung is a different interest with its own page. 2. Each need carries three scores and they never borrow from each other. Drive = Linkage Accuracy x Validity / 100 is computed from the two and typed nowhere. 3. Validity is never set by who holds the need, how many of them there are, how much power they have, or how loudly they assert it. Power does not appear on this page as a score. 4. Scores stay [bracketed] until the engine reads them from the tables. The Alibi Test: Does the Record Bear Out Each Need?

A neighbourhood association opposes a 60-unit apartment building. Its letter says traffic. Check the record. Last year it supported a supermarket that generates more car trips than the apartments would. Two years ago it opposed a townhouse project that came with a parking garage. "Traffic" predicts one of those three positions. "Current homeowners need to protect the value of their homes" predicts all three, and wins, not because anyone read the members' minds but because it explains the behavior with fewer exceptions. Run the same test on the group's opponents. Neither finding is an insult; a prediction that fails is a motive misread. The debate under each need on its own page is about this group's behavior:

The Mirror Test: Six Criteria

"I need the street in front of my house kept free of other people's cars." Real? Certainly. Valid? Hold it up to a mirror. Would you accept the same rule enforced on you by your neighbor? And if everyone claimed the street in front of their house, where would anyone park? The need is real and fails anyway. "I need to get to work in under an hour" passes both tests. Only one of these is a need a resolution should be built around, and it is not the louder one. Each need's validity page argues it against these six, and never against the size or power of the group.

CriterionThe questionA need fails it when
ReciprocityWould the group accept this need honored for its opponents?It wants a rule for others it would reject for itself
Universal testWhat happens if everyone pursues it?Universal pursuit makes it impossible for anyone, or guarantees perpetual conflict
Asks no loss from othersCan it be met without someone else losing?It is satisfied only at another party's expense, or it is a claim on other people's property or choices
Alternative satisfactionCan the underlying need be met another way?Substitutes exist and the group rejects them, which usually means the stated need is not the real one
Scope and reversibilityHow many people, how badly, and can it be undone?Ignoring it costs little, spreads thin, or is easily reversed
Unmet and sharedIs the need actually unmet, and does anyone outside the asking group recognize it?The status quo already satisfies it, or it is held by few and recognized nowhere else

Validity never absorbs feasibility either: how hard a need is to satisfy, and what the group can do without a deal, are read on their own track. Cross either line and the engine rebuilds "might makes right" inside the machine built to replace it.

Where a Need Starts: the Maslow Rungs

Maslow sets the opening price; the arguments do the trading. Every need starts from the band of the rung it serves and the argument tree moves it from there. The same need can appear on several rungs depending on how it is written: "my family needs to eat" is physiological; "my family needs to eat at the restaurant our neighbors cannot afford" is esteem, and the mirror test treats them very differently.

RungOpening priorNeeds written as needs
Physiological85 to 100People need food, water, shelter, sleep and medical care. Workers need wages that cover them. Patients need treatment they can reach.
Safety70 to 85Protection from violence, crime and war; an income they can count on, savings that hold their value, a legal order that applies tomorrow the way it did today. Financial needs sit here: "homeowners need to protect the value of their homes", "renters need a lease that cannot be cancelled on a whim".
Belonging50 to 70A family, a community, a congregation, a country and a team to belong to. Every form of group solidarity. The need to belong is valid; what the group does to outsiders is a separate need, scored separately, and often below the floor.
Esteem40 to 60To be respected, heard and credited. Officials need to be seen as honest; voters need to be answered; workers need their effort recognized. Status needs fail the mirror test: status is scarce by definition.
Self-actualization30 to 50To grow, create, learn, worship and choose for themselves. Low prior, not low value: these lose to survival when the two collide and win on their own arguments when they do not.
Curiosity and play30 to 50To understand, to be entertained, to feel excitement. Explains motives the political ones miss (conspiracy theories as thrill and belonging; voting for disruption because watching the system break is more interesting). The need is real; the vehicle is scored on the belief page.
Below the floor0 to 20Not a rung. Needs that fail the criteria outright: domination (our group needs the other group kept down), tribal winning (we need them to lose), pretext (a belonging need worn as a disguise), and manufactured needs that exist only if a false claim is true.

The bands are priors, not verdicts, and not a lookup table. An esteem need finishes above a safety need when its arguments are stronger.

Bad Motives Are Scored, Not Deleted

The most useful row in any interest table is the one that is real and illegitimate. In the government-ethics beliefs the opponents' list includes "some officeholders want to keep using their position and their families to make money." Its Drive ties for highest on that side; its validity is the lowest on the page. It ranks first as an explanation of behavior and last as something a resolution should honor. That is the information, and it is why this page keeps every need filed for the group, including the ones the group would rather not see written down. The same move must be made against the groups you favour, or the page is propaganda: "our side needs to win the next election" belongs on both lists of any partisan belief, with the same low validity on both.

Where the Scores Go

Two sisters want the same orange; the parent cuts it in half; one needed juice, the other peel. Positions collided; needs never did. The scores on this page are what that arithmetic reads, on every belief page that lists one of this group's needs.

OutputHow the scores set it
Shared interestsNeeds on opposite sides whose statements say the same thing (paired by similarity, then confirmed or disputed by people), with validity at or above the Resolution Floor of 70 on both sides; the pair's combined validity is the harmonic mean. Compromise is built here first.
Primary Conflict PairThe highest-Drive need on each side that is not shared, staged head to head: the one disagreement actually driving the debate. Drive uses validity on purpose: a pretext cannot be the primary conflict, and neither can a saintly need nobody is moved by.
Solution scoresA candidate solution earns, for each need it satisfies, the fraction of the need it meets (0 to 1) times the need's validity, summed per side; the overall score is 2 x S x O / (S + O). Half an orange each: 0.40. Peel to the baker and juice to the drinker: 0.80. The whole orange to one sister: zero, because a solution that satisfies only one side has resolved nothing. Needs below the floor earn a solution nothing even when it satisfies them. No need counts extra because the group behind it is powerful.
Argument importanceEvery reason on a belief page speaks to one or more needs, and its importance is set by the most valid need it genuinely bears on: validity x how directly the reason bears on it, taking the largest. A true fact that matters to nobody weighs nothing.
Who gains, who paysEvery cost and benefit on a belief page names the need that collects it or pays it. A cost always has an address, and this page is where the address resolves to people.
