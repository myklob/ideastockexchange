# Useful phrasings and examples

Started on 2026-11-14 from the fact re-check of 3,156 published rows. Add to it; do not remove entries.

## Named failure modes of a proposed correction

The refute check dropped 51 of 184 proposed corrections. Each was dropped for one of these reasons, and none of them
makes a row wrong:

- **Inside the row's own hedge.** The row says Nixon owed "roughly $465,000"; the committee's figure is $476,431. A gap
  of 2.4 percent under "roughly" is not an error.
- **A reading the row does not make.** "No bribery-for-pardon prosecution has ever been brought", filed under a belief
  about presidential pardons, is not answered by a RICO case about a state governor's pardons.
- **Only a sharper citation.** A wrong author order, "Planning Commission" for "Plan Commission", or a survey "reported
  in 2023" that was first published in 2022. Fix the citation field; do not file a reason to disagree.
- **Leaving out the fact that restores the row.** "LeBron James has more international hardware than Michael Jordan"
  is not answered by Jordan's four golds when LeBron also has four and two bronzes.
- **A counting choice.** "The Supreme Court has twice said Congress cannot limit the pardon power" is not wrong because
  a third opinion says it in passing.
- **Ties the record.** A graduation rate of 90 percent that equals the highest ever reported is still "a record high".
- **Wrong source, right claim.** When a row's claim holds but its cited source never says it, the row needs a source
  fix, not a correction readers could take as saying the claim is false.

## What a good correction looks like

One checkable sentence with the right figure, date or name, which grants what the row gets right and names its
source. Filed examples:

- "Ranked-choice voting reduces but does not eliminate the spoiler effect: in Burlington's 2009 mayoral election,
  removing the losing candidate Wright would have changed the winner from Kiss to Montroll."
- "U.S. life expectancy fell from 78.7 years in 2015 to 78.6 in 2017, and fell sharply from 78.8 in 2019 to 76.4 in
  2021 before rising to 77.5 in 2022."
- "Colorado River hydropower is sold regionally, not across the whole country: Hoover Dam's power is for use in
  Nevada, Arizona and California."

## Row-writing habits the re-check argues for

- **Date every figure** ("as of October 2026"). Stale snapshots were a common error: Ukraine aid given as "about $200
  billion", a 2023 total, when the cumulative figure had grown to about $280 billion.
- **Name the primary source, not the outlet that repeated it.** The Eras Tour attendance figure came from Swift's
  touring company; the row credited Pollstar, which published a different number.
- **A work's bridge says what the work argues.** A book review cited for a critique of physical play never discusses
  physical play.
- **Universal words fail most.** "Every", "no other", "never" and "most" were behind many corrections (independents
  are shut out of most primaries: 17 states at most). State the scope (the Open Era, US metro areas, 2024 arrests).
